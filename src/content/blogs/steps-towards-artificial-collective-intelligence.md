In the foreseeable future, AI agents could far outnumber people if intelligence becomes cheap enough for everyone to have one or more agents of their own. Even agents running the same backbone model could behave very differently because they each have a different context. How would hundreds of millions of these agents interact with one another, as people do in a society? Current multi-agent research rarely asks what happens at that scale.

I think it’s high time to build on what we know about multi-agent systems and study how artificial collective intelligence might emerge at much larger scales. This essay, which I co-wrote with [Yilun Du](https://yilundu.github.io/), [Zhenting Qi](https://zhentingqi.github.io/), [Baiyu Zhu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/baiyu-zhu-9a66b633a/), [Guowei Xu](https://guoweixu.com/), and other friends, offers a comprehensive survey of current research and shares our thoughts on how to start answering that question.
